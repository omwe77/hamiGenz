# Nepal Driving License Application

## Eligibility

You are generally eligible to apply for a Nepal driving license if:

- You are a **Nepali citizen** or a legal resident with a valid visa/residency permit.
- You meet the **minimum age** for the category:
  - **Motorcycle (M):** 16 years (or as specified by Traffic Police).
  - **Light motor vehicle (B):** 18 years.
  - **Medium/heavy (C) / Heavy (D):** 20 years with a valid light license for at least 6 months.
- You have a **valid medical fitness certificate** (if required for your category).
- You have **not had your license suspended/revoked** (unless reinstated).

## Documents required

Common documents for a driving license application:

- **Passport-sized photograph** (recent, white background).
- **Citizenship certificate** or **NID card** (for identity verification).
- **Father's/mother's name** — as it appears on the identity document.
- **Date of birth** — proof (birth certificate, NID, or passport).
- **Address proof** — any document showing your permanent address.
- **Medical fitness certificate** — if required for the category.
- **Application form** — available at Traffic Police offices or online.
- **Application fee** — as posted by the Traffic Police (fee amounts change; verify with your local office).

## Application process

### Step 1: Check eligibility

Confirm you meet the minimum age, category, and medical requirements.

### Step 2: Gather documents

Collect all required documents. Ensure names, dates of birth, and addresses are consistent across all documents.

### Step 3: Visit the Traffic Police office

Go to the **District Traffic Police Office** (जिल्ला ट्रैफिक पुलिस कार्यालय) or the office that serves your area. Bring all original documents + copies.

### Step 4: Medical test (if required)

Undergo the vision test and, where required, the medical fitness examination.

### Step 5: Written / computerized test

Pass the test on **traffic rules and road signs** (in Nepali and/or English). The test is multiple-choice and covers:

- Traffic signals and signs (नियमन संकेत).
- Road markings and rules.
- Right-of-way rules.
- Speed limits and penalties.
- Vehicle safety requirements.

### Step 6: Practical driving test

Pass a **practical driving test** on public roads with a Traffic Police examiner. You must demonstrate:

- Starting, stopping, and shifting.
- Turning, signaling, and lane changing.
- Parking and overtaking.
- Handling intersections and roundabouts.
- Responding to road signs and signals.

### Step 7: Payment of fee

Pay the application fee at the office (cash/card as accepted). Fee amounts change — verify with your local office.

### Step 8: License issuance

After passing all tests and fee payment, the license is issued. The card is typically delivered to you or mailed to your address.

## Important notes

- **Name and date of birth must be consistent** across all documents. Discrepancies cause rejection.
- **Bring originals + photocopies** of all documents.
- **Age proof** is critical — the license will be suspended if issued to someone under the minimum age.
- **Medical fitness** is mandatory for categories requiring it.
- **Renewal** — licenses expire (usually 5 years); renew before expiry to avoid penalties.
- **Lost/stolen license** — report to the Traffic Police office and apply for a replacement.
- **Online application** — some Traffic Police offices accept online applications; availability varies by district.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Wrong category** — applying for a heavy-vehicle license before holding the required light license.
- **Missing documents** — always bring originals + copies.
- **Wrong date format** — Nepal uses DD/MM/YYYY; do not write MM/DD/YYYY.
- **Inconsistent names** — names must match your identity document exactly.
- **Expired documents** — citizenship/NID must be valid at the time of application.
- **Skipping the medical test** — this is a legal requirement, not optional.
